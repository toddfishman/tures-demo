Colophon · the strategic brief

An AI concierge that books the trip you describe.

Not researches one for you.

The wedge

Tures is an executor, not a search engine. You describe the trip in plain English; it books every leg — flight by flight, hotel by hotel, dinner by dinner — to your exact specs, and tells you the truth when reality doesn't cooperate.

Every other "AI travel" product ends with a list of links the human still has to click. Tures ends with confirmation numbers in your inbox.

Who is Andy Rockwell?

Not "the luxury traveler" — that's a marketing segment. Andy is a specific person:

  • Mid-to-late 30s, $300K+ HHI, in finance, tech, law, or healthcare. 12–25 personal travel nights a year, plus business.
  • Knows hotels by name. He didn't ask for "a boutique hotel in Paris." He asked for Real Christine. He built the Lapland leg around Javri Lodge.
  • Status traveler. Airline status, Amex Platinum or Centurion, maybe a Virtuoso advisor he tolerates more than likes.
  • Friction-allergic. Wants to spend eleven minutes describing the trip and never think about logistics again until he's in the lounge.
  • High-trust if earned. Will hand Tures his card, loyalty numbers, calendar — and fire it in 30 seconds if it fakes a confirmation or misses a non-stop he asked for.

The conversion thesis: Andy isn't a price shopper. He's a time-and-cognition shopper. Charge him for the time and he'll pay it.

Jobs to be done

  1. Book exactly what I told you. If I name the hotel, you book that hotel. No "we found something close."
  2. Find the cleanest feasible alternative when my ask is impossible — and ask before committing. Default on uncertainty: pause and ask, don't autopilot.
  3. Handle the chain of permissions. Use my Amex, credit my Finnair number, apply my status — and tell me what you did.
  4. Coordinate dinner reservations — including the ones without a public API.
  5. Take care of things while I sleep. Schedule change, dinner cancellation, weather, a strike three days out — handle it and tell me what you did.

Where Tures sits: the empty quadrant — executor-grade automation at concierge-grade taste. The defensibility isn't the LLM; it's the integration surface (Duffel + Booking partners + Resy + concierge inventory + card vault + loyalty graph), the Taste Engine's per-user model, and the trust UX — the live dashboard and the Hiccup Handler that let Andy see Tures reason before he believes it.

Business model

Hybrid. $1,800/year for unlimited trips + transparent supplier commissions disclosed in the receipt. No hidden markup; often saves money vs. retail. Slots between Amex Platinum ($695/yr) and a working relationship with a top Virtuoso advisor.

Honest skepticism

  • Top-end restaurant inventory isn't solvable by software alone. Resy + OpenTable + SevenRooms cover maybe 60% of the urban dining graph. Be honest about autobook vs. request-and-confirm.
  • Anti-bot defenses on luxury hotel sites. Direct bookings mean browser automation against Cloudflare and friends. Plan for it; don't promise full coverage day one.
  • Card-on-file is PCI-DSS scope. Tokenization vault (Stripe-as-vault) is a week-1 decision.
  • The Hiccup Handler must never cry wolf. Acting on probability is the whole point — and the whole risk. It surfaces a held fix; it executes on a real trigger; it explains itself every time.
  • Trust collapses on a single visible failure. Never confirm what you can't verify in two places. No fake success states — ever.
  • The LLM never books anything itself. It produces a structured plan; a deterministic executor validates and submits. The model parses and explains; it doesn't book.
